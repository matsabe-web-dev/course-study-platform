5 Beginner Mistakes That Slow Down Your First HTML Project

August 12, 2026

Every student makes these mistakes at least once — and every single one of them is easy to fix once you know what to look for. Here's what trips up beginners most in their first few weeks.

1. Reaching for a <div> before checking if a real tag exists

It's the fastest habit to fall into: wrap everything in a <div>, style it, move on. But HTML already has tags for most of what you're building — <nav>, <header>, <article>, <button>. Using them isn't a style preference; it's what lets screen readers, search engines, and your own future self actually understand the page.

2. Skipping the alt attribute, or filling it with nothing useful

An empty alt attribute — or one that just says "image" — helps no one. It should describe what the image is showing and why it's there, the same way you'd describe it out loud to someone who can't see the screen.

3. Nesting elements that don't belong together

Putting a <div> inside a <p>, or a heading inside a <span>, will often still render — browsers are forgiving — but it breaks the underlying structure silently. The page looks fine and works badly, which is the worst kind of bug because nothing tells you it's there.

4. Writing IDs and classes that describe appearance, not purpose

Naming something "red-box" works until the box isn't red anymore. Naming it "warning-message" survives every redesign that follows, because it describes what the element is, not what it currently looks like.

5. Never validating the markup

Most beginners never run their HTML through a validator until something breaks. Doing it early — even on a rough first draft — catches unclosed tags and structural mistakes before they turn into hours of confused debugging later.

None of these mistakes mean you're bad at this. They mean you're doing exactly what every developer before you did in their first few weeks. The only difference between a beginner and someone six months ahead is how many of these they've already caught.
