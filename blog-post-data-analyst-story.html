From Data Analyst Student to Job Offer in 4 Months

July 28, 2026

Faith Njeri graduated from our Data Analytics track in March. Four months later, she had a full-time offer. We asked her what actually made the difference.

"I didn't think I was a numbers person"

Faith came into the program from a background in customer support, with no formal background in data or statistics. "I almost didn't sign up because I assumed I wasn't the kind of person who does data analytics," she says. "I thought you needed to already be good at maths. What I actually needed was to be good at asking questions of a dataset — that's a very different skill, and it's one you build."

The turning point: building dashboards for a real problem

Faith says the shift happened around week six, when the class moved from exercises to building a dashboard around an actual, messy dataset. "Clean, prepared data in a tutorial doesn't teach you anything about the job. The real work is dealing with data that's missing half its values and figuring out what story it's still able to tell. That's the point it stopped feeling academic and started feeling like the actual job."

What she thinks got her the offer

By the time she started applying, Faith had three dashboard projects built around real, public datasets — not just exercises. "In every interview, I wasn't describing what I'd learned. I was walking them through a decision I made in a real project and why. That's a completely different conversation than reciting a syllabus."

Her advice to students starting the track now: "Don't wait until you feel ready to build something real. I built my first dashboard while I still felt like I barely understood pivot tables. That project ended up being the one I talked about most in interviews."

Faith now works as a data analyst for a logistics company, building the same kind of dashboards she once found intimidating.
