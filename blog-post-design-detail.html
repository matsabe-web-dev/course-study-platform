The One Design Detail Most Beginners Miss (And Every Recruiter Notices)

August 19, 2026

Ask a beginner what makes a design look "professional," and they'll usually point at colour — a nice palette, a trendy gradient, maybe a bold accent colour. Ask a hiring manager who's screened a hundred portfolios what actually separates the strong ones from the rest, and they'll almost never mention colour at all. They'll mention spacing.

It's not what you add. It's what you leave alone.

Consistent spacing — between elements, around text, inside buttons — is the detail that makes an interface feel calm and deliberate instead of cramped and improvised. Beginners tend to treat spacing as an afterthought, nudging things around until they "look okay" on one screen size. Professionals treat it as a system: a small set of spacing values, reused everywhere, so the whole interface feels like it was designed by one hand.

You can spot the difference in seconds. A beginner's layout has fourteen slightly different gaps between elements. A professional's layout has three or four, repeated consistently. Nothing about the colours or the fonts changed — but one looks intentional and the other doesn't.

Why recruiters notice this first

When someone reviewing your portfolio has thirty seconds per project, they're not analysing your colour theory. They're pattern-matching against interfaces they already trust — and inconsistent spacing is the fastest tell that a design was assembled rather than built. It reads as inexperience even when everything else about the project is solid.

The fix isn't complicated, which is exactly why it's worth fixing early. Pick a small spacing scale — something like 8px, 16px, 24px, 32px — and force yourself to only use those values. It feels restrictive for the first project. By the third, it's second nature, and your layouts start looking like they belong on a real product instead of a tutorial.
